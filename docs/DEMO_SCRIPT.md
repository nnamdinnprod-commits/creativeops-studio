# Demo Script — 5 Minutes

**Status: skeleton. Claude Code completes this in Phase 8 with real project names, real
numbers from the seed data, and the actual click path.**

The demo has one job: make a Creative Operations leader think *this person understands how
a studio actually runs*. Feature tours do not achieve that. A single traced thread does.

## Opening — 20 seconds

State plainly what this is: an independent prototype, fictional data, built in about a day
with AI-assisted development, exploring how creative intelligence and production planning
could connect. Say it up front. Overclaiming is the only way to lose the room.

## 1. Dashboard — 40 seconds

Open on "what needs attention". Three items, each naming a project and a cause. The point:
a producer's first ten minutes of the day are usually spent reconstructing this by hand.

## 2. Pipeline — 30 seconds

Show the board. Then attempt to move the incomplete brief into In Production and let it be
refused with a reason. The refusal is the interesting part — it is operational policy made
mechanical.

## 3. Resource conflict — 50 seconds

Show Alex at 95% against a Friday deadline. Point out the arithmetic is deterministic, not
model output. Then show the conflict list.

## 4. AI recommendation and approval — 50 seconds

Show the reassignment recommendation with its rationale and impact. **Reject it once** to
demonstrate that rejection is a real state and stays in history. Then accept it, and show
the capacity figures moving. This is the "humans stay in control" argument, demonstrated
rather than asserted.

## 5. Brief assistant — 50 seconds

Paste the messy brief. Show the extraction, then the readiness score with its rubric
visible. Emphasise that the score explains *what each gap blocks* — this is the "identify
missing information" competence from the job description, made systematic. Create the
project.

## 6. Creative intelligence to production — 70 seconds

**The centrepiece — give it the most time.**

Show the German lifestyle-vs-product performance gap. Then the production recommendation:
three variants, two days, Maya has the window, German copy review required, awaiting
approval. Read the caveat about sample size aloud — the restraint is part of the argument.

Accept it. Show the project appearing in the pipeline with its assignment and localisation
row attached.

Say the line explicitly: *creative intelligence tells you what is happening; creative
operations decides what to do about it. This is the bridge.*

## 7. Localisation — 30 seconds

Show the French bottleneck: no reviewer assigned, four days to deadline, flagged at risk on
the dashboard. Localisation as part of the production workflow, not a downstream afterthought.

## 8. Close — 30 seconds

The proposed chain versus the traditional one. The point is not removing the producer — it
is giving the producer better information and more time for the decisions only a human
should make.

---

## Presenting notes

- Run from a clean seeded database. Reset before each run.
- Mock mode is fine and removes network risk. Have a key configured only if you intend to
  show a live call, and if you do, show it on the brief assistant where latency is expected.
- If asked what you would build next: real integration behind the existing mock boundary,
  historical capacity data to make estimates learned rather than assumed, and authentication.
  Naming what is missing reads as judgement.
- If asked how much of this you wrote: answer honestly. AI-assisted development is the
  subject of the demonstration, not something to be sheepish about. What you are showing is
  the specification, the operational logic, and the judgement about where AI belongs.
